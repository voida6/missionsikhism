## What happened

[Operation Blue Star](/events/operation-blue-star) — the Indian Army's assault on the Harmandir Sahib complex in June 1984 — was followed on 31 October by the assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, shot at her residence by two of her own bodyguards, both Sikhs.

Violence against Sikhs began in Delhi that evening and ran for roughly three days. Sikh men were dragged from homes, buses and trains and killed, frequently by burning; Sikh houses, shops and gurdwaras were destroyed; Sikh women were raped. It occurred in other Indian cities as well, but Delhi was the centre of it.{{1,2}}

The official figure, established by the Nanavati Commission and cited by Human Rights Watch, is **2,733 killed in Delhi**, with hundreds more elsewhere. Sikh organisations and some human rights groups put the national figure closer to 4,000.{{2,3}}

## Riot, or something else

What to call it is not a matter of style, and this site does not use "riots" without qualification.

A riot is spontaneous. The commissions of inquiry — ten of them over three decades — repeatedly found patterns inconsistent with that: attackers arriving in organised groups, supplied with kerosene, working from voter and ration lists that identified Sikh households, over a period in which the police in Delhi were largely absent or complicit. Human Rights Watch's assessment records evidence of complicity by both the police and by leaders of the governing Congress Party.{{2}}

Those findings support the terms Sikhs generally use — *pogrom*, *massacre*, *organised violence*. Indian official usage remains "riots". The disagreement is about whether the killing was organised, and the evidence on that question is not evenly balanced.

## The accountability record

This is the part that keeps the events of 1984 politically live rather than historical, and it can be stated in figures.{{2}}

- 587 complaints were formally registered in Delhi. Police closed **241 of them without investigation**.
- Ten government commissions and committees were appointed across three decades.
- Around **30 people have been convicted**, most of them low-ranking.
- **No police officer has been convicted.**
- Despite widespread sexual violence, there have been **no rape prosecutions**.

Convictions of senior political figures only began to arrive after 2018, thirty-four years on, and remain few.

## Why it belongs in this chronicle

Sikh history contains a long list of episodes in which the community was attacked for being what it is, and this site records them from [1606](/events/martyrdom-of-guru-arjan) onward. November 1984 differs from the older ones in one respect that matters: it happened inside a functioning constitutional democracy with courts, a free press and a police force, and that machinery produced almost no accountability for it.

For a great many Sikhs, in India and in the diaspora, the failure to prosecute has done more lasting damage than the killing — because it is ongoing, and because it is the state's own record rather than anyone's account of it.
