## A village, a family, a year

Nanak was born in 1469 in Rai Bhoi Ki Talwandi, a village some forty miles south-west of Lahore, to Kalu, a Bedi Khatri who kept the revenue accounts for the local chief, and his wife Tripta.{{1,4}} The year is one of the very few biographical facts about the Guru that no serious historian disputes.{{1}}

The detail is worth holding onto, because it sets the social frame for everything after. Kalu was a *patwari* — a village revenue official — and Nanak's father-in-law, Mula Chona, worked for a Jat village chief.{{2}} The family sat inside the administrative machinery of the late Delhi Sultanate, not outside it — the literate service class on which Punjab's revenue system ran.{{3}} Talwandi itself had been founded by Rai Bhoi, a high-caste Hindu convert to Islam, so the village Nanak grew up in had a mosque near its centre and a temple among the Hindu quarters.{{2}} He was raised in the ordinary religious plurality of fifteenth-century Punjab, and knew from childhood what it looked like from the inside.

## The month nobody can settle

His Gurpurab is observed on the full moon of Kattak — October or November. A substantial body of scholarship says he was actually born in Vaisakh, in April.

The Kattak date rests largely on the Bala Janam-sakhi, which reports a horoscope obtained from Nanak's uncle Lalu.{{6}} In 1913 the historian Karam Singh published *Katak ki Visakh*, arguing that the Bala tradition was unreliable, that "Bala" was probably not a companion of the Guru at all, and that the older manuscripts point to Vaisakh.{{6}} The argument has never gone away. When Punjabi University marked the quincentenary in 1969, the conference was timed to the Kattak observance while the special journal issue released at it argued for 15 April 1469 — the two positions printed side by side, in the same week, by the same institution.{{2}} As Gurinder Singh Mann observes, a generation of scholars has come and gone since, and no clarity has emerged.{{2}}

There is also a record of practice changing. As late as 1815, under Ranjit Singh, the birth festival at Nankana Sahib was held in April; the November observance became standard afterwards.{{5}} This site follows the community's Kattak observance for the Gurpurab, and reports the year — not the month — as historically settled.

## What the childhood stories are, and are not

The janam-sakhis give Nanak a childhood of visible signs: the boy who refuses the sacred thread, the schoolboy who turns his letters toward God, the herdsman accused of letting his buffaloes into a neighbour's wheat and made to answer to the village elders.{{2}} His elder sister, Bebe Nanaki, is remembered as the first to recognise what he was.

How much of this is history is the central methodological quarrel in Sikh studies. McLeod sorted 124 janam-sakhi episodes into possible, probable, improbable and impossible, and concluded that as biography the texts were "highly unsatisfactory".{{1}} Mann's reply is that this measures the wrong thing: the earliest layer, the Puratan tradition, was written when people who had met Nanak may still have been alive, and it preserves a man who bathed, ate, farmed and had sons who did not always obey him — which makes it evidence, if read with attention to when each text was composed.{{2}} The buffalo story is a fair example. Nothing supernatural happens in it; what it records is a teenager summoned before the *panch* and *chaudhari*, the everyday texture of village authority.{{2}}

Read that way, the childhood accounts are not proof of miracles and were never offered as scripture. They are what the earliest community chose to remember about its founder — a different kind of fact, and still a useful one.

## The village becomes a place of pilgrimage

Talwandi was renamed [Nankana Sahib](/places/nankana-sahib) in the Guru's honour, and Gurdwara Janam Asthan now stands on the traditional birth site.{{7}} The town has been in Pakistan since the [Partition of 1947](/events/partition-of-punjab), which put the birthplace of the faith on the far side of a closed border for most of the following century — one reason the [opening of the Kartarpur corridor in 2019](/events/kartarpur-corridor-opens) mattered as much as it did.

His birth opens [Era 1 — the Guru Period](/history/guru-period). The faith itself begins about thirty years later, [at Sultanpur](/events/enlightenment-at-sultanpur).
