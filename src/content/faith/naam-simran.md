## The practice

*Naam* is the divine Name; *simran* is remembrance. Together they name the practice that the Guru Granth Sahib returns to more insistently than any other: holding the awareness of the divine continuously, in speech, in thought, and while doing something else entirely.

It takes several forms — the recitation of *[Waheguru](/glossary/waheguru)*, the reading of the [daily prayers](/nitnem), and the singing of scripture as [kirtan](/glossary/kirtan) — but the forms are not the point. The point is the attention.{{1,2,3,4}}

## Why it sits at the centre

Sikh teaching does not treat the world as an obstacle to be escaped. There is no monasticism in it, no requirement of celibacy, no withdrawal; [Guru Nanak](/people/guru-nanak) spent his last years farming at [Kartarpur](/events/founding-of-kartarpur) rather than in retreat, and the community he left was one of householders.

That creates a problem the practice answers. If the spiritual life is not somewhere else, it has to be conducted here — in work, family, obligation and noise. *Naam simran* is the mechanism: not time set aside from ordinary life, but a quality of attention carried through it.

This is why the tradition pairs it so tightly with *[seva](/glossary/seva)*, service, and with honest labour. The three are usually stated together — remember the Name, earn by your own effort, share what you have — and they are meant as one instruction rather than three.{{1,2,3}}

## What it is not

It is not a technique for producing experiences. The Gurus are consistently unimpressed by austerities, spectacular disciplines and displays of attainment, and the janam-sakhi accounts of Guru Nanak's encounters with yogis turn on exactly that point.

Nor is it mechanical repetition. The scripture is direct about the emptiness of recitation performed without the attention it exists to hold — a criticism it applies to Sikh practice as readily as to anyone else's.{{1,2,3}}

## Its object

What is being remembered is the reality set out in the opening line of the scripture and in [One God](/faith/one-god): one, without form, not born and not dying, present in creation rather than outside it. Remembrance of that is not devotion to something distant. It is attention to what is already the case, which the tradition holds we spend most of our lives failing to notice.{{1,2,3}}
