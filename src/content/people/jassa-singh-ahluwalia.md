## Command

Jassa Singh was born in 1718 and trained by [Nawab Kapur Singh](/people/nawab-kapur-singh), who handed him the command of the [Dal Khalsa](/events/formation-of-the-dal-khalsa) when it was constituted in 1748. He held it, in practice, until his death thirty-five years later.

The period he commanded through is the one in which the Sikhs went from being hunted to holding the Punjab. He led at the [rescue of Abdali's captives at the Goindwal ferry](/events/rescue-at-goindwal) in 1761, was in the column caught at Kup in [the Vadda Ghallughara](/events/vadda-ghallughara) in 1762 and survived it wounded, took [Sirhind](/events/sikh-capture-of-sirhind) in 1764, held [Lahore](/events/sikh-capture-of-lahore) in 1765, and was among the leaders who [rode into Delhi](/events/sikh-occupation-of-delhi) in 1783.

## The title, and its limits

When the Sikhs took Lahore the Panth conferred on him the title *Sultan-ul-Qaum* — king of the nation.

It is worth being precise about what that did and did not mean, because it is easy to read as a coronation and it was not one. The Dal Khalsa was a confederacy whose binding decisions were taken by the Sarbat Khalsa as *gurmatas*; a supreme commander executed those decisions and did not replace them. He struck coin at Lahore in the Gurus' names, not his own — the same formula [Banda Singh Bahadur had used](/events/battle-of-chappar-chiri) and that [Ranjit Singh would use](/events/ranjit-singh-proclaimed-maharaja) after him.

He was, in other words, the head of an army answerable to an assembly, given a royal title by that assembly and never permitted to behave as though it made him a king. That distinction is the whole difference between the misl period and the monarchy that replaced it.

## Afterwards

He died at Amritsar on 23 October 1783, the year of the Delhi expedition. The Ahluwalia misl he headed became the state of Kapurthala.

Sixteen years after his death, [Ranjit Singh took Lahore](/events/ranjit-singh-captures-lahore) and began absorbing the confederacy into a single monarchy — more powerful than what Jassa Singh had commanded, and [gone within a decade of its founder's death](/events/death-of-ranjit-singh).
