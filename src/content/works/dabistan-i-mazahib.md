The *Dabistan* was compiled in Persian around the 1640s and 1650s by an author
who travelled widely and wrote about each community with unusual curiosity
rather than polemic. Its chapter on the Nanak-panthis — the Sikhs — describes
the community during the lifetime of the sixth and seventh Gurus, from outside
the tradition and before any Sikh historical writing existed.

That makes it a control on the Sikh sources for the seventeenth century, which
is a rare and valuable thing.

Its author had direct contact with Guru Hargobind and his successor, which makes
him a non-Sikh eyewitness to the armed Panth within the sixth Guru's own
lifetime — the single most useful thing about him.

**Which translation matters here.** The link above is the Shea and Troyer
English version (Oriental Translation Fund, 1843), because it is the one freely
readable online. It is not the one to rely on: it was made with insufficient
Persian, and the chapter on the Nanak-panthis is among the worst affected. That
section was later retranslated by Sardar Umrao Singh Majithia and by Ganda
Singh.

Where this site cites the *Dabistan* on a point of substance, it follows Irfan
Habib's translation rather than Shea and Troyer.
