summary: "The major Sikh chronicle of the eighteenth century, composed in Punjabi verse by the grandson of a participant. It is the tradition's own account of the persecutions, the massacres, and the rise of the misls."

Bhangu wrote in Punjabi verse, in 170 episodes, and he was not far from his
material: his grandfather belonged to the generation the chronicle describes.
For the eighteenth century — the ghallugharas, the Dal Khalsa, the misls — it is
the fullest account from inside the community, and much of what later histories
relate about the period traces back to it.

**On its date.** Vir Singh placed its completion in 1841; Gurinder Singh Mann
argues Bhangu likely finished it between 1810 and 1813. The earlier dating
matters, because it would make the chronicle roughly contemporary with Malcolm's
account rather than a generation later.

**On how to read it.** It is a chronicle written to establish a case — composed
in part to answer British enquiries about who the Sikhs were and by what right
they held the Punjab — and it is openly hostile to Banda Singh Bahadur,
reflecting the later Tat Khalsa position. Its testimony is indispensable and it
is not neutral.
