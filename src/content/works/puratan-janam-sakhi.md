The *janam-sakhis* are the birth-stories: collections of episodes from Guru
Nanak's life, compiled well after it, and shaped by what the community wanted to
remember rather than by any documentary impulse. The *Puratan* recension, edited
by Bhai Vir Singh, is the earliest surviving of them and the one this site cites
most often.

It has to be used, because there is nothing else. Almost everything anyone knows
about Guru Nanak's life — Talwandi, the granary at Sultanpur, the disappearance
into the Bein, the journeys — reaches us through this tradition.

**How hard it can be pressed** is the question W. H. McLeod's *Guru Nanak and
the Sikh Religion* (1968) put to the sources, and the answer was: considerably
less hard than had been assumed. That book caused real offence when it appeared
and its method is now largely absorbed. Gurinder Singh Mann's later appraisal
sets out what the sources will still bear.

Where this site states something from the janam-sakhis as fact, it is because
the point survives that scrutiny. Where it does not, the entry says so.
