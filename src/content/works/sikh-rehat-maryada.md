The *Rehat Maryada* is the modern answer to a question the Panth had argued over
since the eighteenth century: what, concretely, does the [Khalsa](/glossary/khalsa)
discipline require? Earlier *rahit-namas* — codes attributed to Sikhs close to
Guru Gobind Singh — differ from one another, and the differences were live
disputes rather than curiosities.

What makes this document unusual is how it was arrived at. It was worked out
across roughly fifteen years by committee and consultation across the Panth,
rather than issued by an authority, and it takes positions: on
[initiation](/glossary/amrit), on who may perform which functions, on what may
and may not be done in a [gurdwara](/glossary/gurdwara), and on practices it
regards as having no place in Sikh life.

It is cited on this site as a primary source, because for questions of current
practice it is not a description of what Sikhs do — it is the text that
establishes it.
