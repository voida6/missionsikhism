Malcolm was an East India Company soldier and diplomat, and wrote while the
misl system was still operating and Ranjit Singh's consolidation of it was
recent and incomplete. He is therefore a contemporary witness to an arrangement
that no longer existed by the time historians came to describe it.

He is the earliest English source to describe the *gurmata*, which he calls the
"state council" and locates at Amritsar, and he grasps its character: he reads
the Sikh polity as a federative republic rather than as a collection of chiefs.
He also records a gurmata called around 1805 that was poorly attended — direct
evidence of how far the confederacy had fragmented by then.

The usual cautions for a Company officer writing about a power his employer was
measuring apply throughout.
