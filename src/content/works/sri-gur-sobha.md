summary: "The earliest surviving Sikh account of Guru Gobind Singh, written in Braj verse by a poet of his own court within a few years of his death. It is the closest thing the tradition has to a contemporary witness of the founding of the Khalsa."
---

Sainapati wrote as a poet attached to Guru Gobind Singh's court, which makes
this the nearest thing to testimony that survives for the events of 1699 and
1708. Everything else written about the tenth Guru's life postdates it, often by
a century or more.

**On its date, which is not settled.** The text states its own completion as
1701, but it narrates events as late as October 1708 — so the 1701 date is
generally read as that of a first draft, with the poem enlarged and finished
around 1711. This site cites it as 1711.

The standard printed edition is Ganda Singh's (Punjabi University, Patiala,
1967). It is worth knowing that Ganda Singh does not identify the manuscript he
worked from, which is a real limit on how hard the text can be pressed.
