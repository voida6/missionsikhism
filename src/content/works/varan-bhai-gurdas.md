Bhai Gurdas served four Gurus and wrote out the
[Adi Granth](/events/installation-of-adi-granth) at Guru Arjan's dictation in
1604. His own *vars* are the first attempt from inside the tradition to set out
what the Gurus taught in expository form, and the earliest near-contemporary
account of Sikh history and institutions.

Guru Arjan is traditionally reported to have called them the key to the Guru's
Word, and that fixes their status exactly: they are not scripture and never
were, and they are the only non-scriptural texts that may be recited in a
[gurdwara](/glossary/gurdwara) alongside it.

For the sixteenth-century Panth there is very little else written from within
it, which is why a line like *Var 1, pauri 45* — "Nanak struck his coin in the
world, and the Nirmal Panth set out" — carries as much weight in Sikh
historiography as it does.
