The argument is a corrective to the way the century is usually narrated. The
rituals, codes and cultural markers associated with the Khalsa are commonly
treated as having been settled by Guru Gobind Singh in 1699 and thereafter
merely defended. Dhavan traces them instead as something worked out slowly,
contested from within, and not composite until close to 1799.

The method is what makes it useful here: she reads the Punjabi chronicles and
the Persian records together, and pays attention to the rural communities who
made up the misls rather than only to their chiefs.

Anyone reading this site's eighteenth-century entries should know this argument
exists, because it cuts against the tidier version of the story.
